# 02 — Experience DNA Interview

**Version:** 0.3  
**Status:** Accepted — Gate 2.1 closed  
**Gate:** 2.1 — Experience Foundation  
**Interview owner:** Raihan  
**Facilitator:** Rei

## Purpose

This interview discovers the authentic source material for Raihan's OS before
selecting an art direction. It does not ask Raihan to choose arbitrary colors,
fonts, or fashionable interface styles. It asks what the system should embody,
what real work should be visible, and where the experience should take one
meaningful creative risk.

## What is already decided

The interview does not reopen the following Phase 1 decisions:

- one Central OS with AI Research and Engineering/Mining gateways;
- two distinct environments inside one persistent system shell;
- a functional terminal with visual navigation equivalents;
- boot sequence, environment switching, reading mode, and meaningful
  interactive explanation;
- desktop-first full experience and simplified functional mobile experience;
- emotional sequence: amazement, curiosity, trust, exploration,
  collaboration;
- progressive depth from accessible summary to technical evidence;
- privacy, confidentiality, accessibility, and scientific integrity as gates;
  and
- no public comments, accounts, or persisted visitor preferences at launch.

## Interview 2A — Experience DNA

Answer freely and use examples. `Belum tahu` is a valid answer; uncertainty will
be recorded rather than silently filled.

### 1. Authentic working world

When you are doing your best AI research or engineering estimation work, what
objects, tools, documents, screens, habits, or physical environments genuinely
surround you? List the details that feel most "Raihan", even if they seem too
ordinary for a website.

### 2. Central OS metaphor

Ignoring visual style for a moment, what should the Central OS fundamentally
feel like?

- a personal command center;
- a research observatory;
- a technical archive;
- an experimental laboratory;
- an engineering control room; or
- something else in your own words?

Explain why.

### 3. First meaningful moment

After the short boot sequence, what is the first meaningful thing a visitor
should be able to *do*, not merely see?

Examples include choosing a world, inspecting a live model, entering a command,
opening a current investigation, or following a question. You may propose
something entirely different.

### 4. Information density

Which default balance feels more like you?

- calm and spacious, revealing detail on demand;
- moderately dense, like a disciplined professional workstation; or
- intentionally dense, like an expert instrument panel.

Should AI Research and Engineering use different density levels?

### 5. Shared invariant

AI Research and Engineering will look and behave differently. What single
quality, visual behavior, or structural rule must remain unmistakably identical
in both so they still feel like one mind?

### 6. Environmental contrast

Describe the contrast between the two worlds without naming colors.

For example: fluid versus structural, probabilistic versus measured, living
signals versus constructed systems, exploratory versus accountable. Which
contrast is truthful, and which contrasts would misrepresent your work?

### 7. One memorable signature

If visitors remember only one interaction or visual behavior from the entire
website, what should it reveal about how you think? It must communicate
something real—not exist only to look impressive.

### 8. Human presence

How much of the person behind the technical work should be visible in the core
experience?

Consider portrait photography, voice, handwriting, desk artifacts, music,
coffee rituals, humor, failures, or personal notes. What feels authentic, and
what would feel performative or too intimate?

### 9. Voice and personality

When the OS speaks through labels, terminal responses, empty states, and error
messages, how should it sound? Give three traits and, if possible, one example
of a sentence that sounds right and one that sounds wrong.

### 10. External cultural references

Outside portfolio websites, which films, games, albums, musicians, books,
architecture, machines, scientific instruments, interfaces, or fictional
worlds have an atmosphere you genuinely love? Explain the specific quality you
love; the goal is inspiration, not imitation.

### 11. Rejection boundary

Besides being generic, what would make you immediately reject a design?
Examples might include excessive neon, fake hacker aesthetics, corporate
minimalism, too much glassmorphism, childish animation, sterile dashboards, or
overly academic presentation.

### 12. Sound and silence

Should sound exist at all? If yes, should it be limited to an optional boot
moment, subtle interface feedback, ambient environment, or a dedicated music
choice? Silence remains the default recommendation unless sound serves a clear
purpose.

## How the answers will be used

Rei will convert the answers into:

1. an Experience Thesis;
2. authentic source-material inventory;
3. shared-system invariants;
4. controlled AI/Engineering contrast rules;
5. three art-direction territories;
6. one recommended signature interaction;
7. voice principles and anti-patterns; and
8. the inputs for Interview 2B and Gate 2.2.

No visual direction will be approved solely because it is attractive. Each
direction must trace back to an answer, a Phase 1 requirement, or a documented
design rationale.

## Response record — 2026-08-15

The following is a normalized record of Raihan's answers. It preserves intent
without treating an inspiration reference as a request to reproduce another
property's visual identity.

1. **Working atmosphere:** focused research in a calm, quiet environment;
   solitude similar to exploring the world of Fallout; old music associated
   with the 1950s and 1960s supports concentration.
2. **Central metaphor:** a command center or personal instrument comparable in
   function and intimacy to a Pip-Boy. Raihan requested additional original
   metaphors before selection.
3. **First moment:** visitors should learn that the interface can be operated,
   including a concise guide to hotkeys and terminal access.
4. **Density:** information-rich but never overwhelming; empty, excessively
   sparse layouts are undesirable. The two environments may use different
   density levels.
5. **Shared invariant:** both worlds must retain an OS-like, personal-instrument
   identity.
6. **Environmental contrast:** delegated to the design lead for a grounded
   recommendation.
7. **Signature quality:** concise, optimal, fast, and straightforward, presented
   through a visually remarkable but purposeful theme.
8. **Human presence:** a silent radio/listening section may route to Spotify.
   Social and professional channels must be integrated as part of the system,
   not displayed as a generic row of platform logos. Other personal material
   remains open.
9. **Audio:** no website sound. Editorial voice still requires confirmation.
10. **Cultural references:** the personal-instrument quality of Fallout's
    Pip-Boy, Evangelion, 1950s–1960s music, and the Neo-Tokyo atmosphere of
    Akira.
11. **Rejection boundary:** no generic pattern-following and no direct Fallout,
    cyberpunk, or other reference imitation. The project must establish its own
    style guide.
12. **Sound policy:** silent interface; external music links may exist but must
    not autoplay.

The design interpretation of these answers is recorded in
`03-experience-foundation-synthesis.md`.
