## 1. Purpose

Gate 2.7 now resolves the decision deliberately deferred by ADR-0002. The name
must emerge from the complete product rather than forcing the product to imitate
a name. This interview defines the human and linguistic bounds needed before
candidate generation, domain screening, and final scoring. On 2026-08-15, the
Product Owner changed the working method from question-first to
candidate-first. Document 40 now provides the controlled first shortlist; the
unanswered questions below remain a validation checklist rather than a blocker
to exploration.

The gate will not select a name merely because a domain is available. It will
select a credible long-term identity, then find the strongest viable domain
architecture for it.

## 2. Fixed evidence the name must carry

The completed Phase 2 system is:

- a personal cognitive field console owned by one real operator;
- precise and direct, serious but not sterile;
- experimental without becoming playful or random;
- a shared chassis for AI Research and Engineering/Mining;
- a system for making complex ideas inspectable;
- structured density with evidence, provenance, limitations, and artifacts;
- culturally informed by field instruments, archival warmth, and metropolitan
  technical scale without imitating Fallout, Evangelion, Akira, cyberpunk, or
  a generic hacker interface; and
- professionally credible in a browser, CV, paper, GitHub profile, conference,
  engineering proposal, email address, and spoken introduction.

The name must survive without the OS interface, animation, color, or logo.

## 3. Design-lead brand-architecture hypothesis

The recommended structure to test is:

```text
[NICKNAME]                         masterbrand / publishing identity
by Raihan [public full-name form]  permanent human attribution
[NICKNAME] OS                      optional product/interface descriptor
├── Signal Lab                     AI Research environment
├── Operations Ledger              Engineering environment
└── Field Notes                    shared journal/editorial layer
```

This gives the nickname genuine brand power while never hiding the accountable
person behind the work. `OS` remains a product descriptor rather than the only
source of distinctiveness. Signal Lab and Operations Ledger remain provisional
public labels until the chosen name proves they fit.

## 4. Interview questions

### A. Nickname origin and ownership

1. **Do you already have one or more nickname candidates?** If yes, write every
   form, spelling, capitalization, pronunciation, origin, and personal meaning.
   If not, explicitly authorize Rei to generate the candidate universe from
   zero.

2. **Must the nickname be recognizably related to `Raihan`, or may it be a fully
   independent word?**  
   **Recommendation:** permit both during exploration, but require permanent
   `by Raihan …` attribution for an independent name.

3. **How personally intimate may the name feel?** Should it sound like a person
   people can call you, a technical callsign, a publishing imprint, or an
   abstract system?  
   **Recommendation:** a name people can comfortably address you by, with enough
   structural character to function as an imprint.

### B. Language and sound

4. **Which pronunciation audience matters most:** Indonesian-first,
   English/international-first, or equally natural in both?  
   **Recommendation:** equally usable in Indonesian and professional English,
   ideally two or three syllables with one obvious pronunciation.

5. **Which tonal qualities should the spoken name carry?** Choose or rank the
   most important: precise, calm, intelligent, technical, human, archival,
   futuristic, severe, elegant, or unusual. Add any prohibited qualities.

6. **Are invented words, altered spellings, initials, numbers, punctuation, or
   non-English roots acceptable?**  
   **Recommendation:** invented words and meaningful roots are allowed;
   reject numbers, punctuation-dependent spelling, decorative Japanese, and
   deliberately difficult orthography.

7. **Are there letters, sounds, languages, religious/cultural associations, or
   meanings that must be included or avoided?** This includes a name that may
   sound good in English but awkward, comic, or negative in Indonesian.

### C. Professional use and attribution

8. **May professionals address you directly by this nickname in email,
   conferences, open-source work, and collaboration calls?** Or should it name
   only the website while people continue to call you Raihan?  
   **Recommendation:** allow direct address; otherwise it is a project title,
   not truly your nickname.

9. **What exact public form of your real name should accompany the brand?** For
   example, `Raihan`, a full legal/professional name, or a shortened professional
   name. This is required for authorship, CV, structured data, citation, and
   search discoverability.

10. **Should the name remain truthful for a one-person platform indefinitely,
    or may it later become the name of a studio/lab/team?**  
    **Recommendation:** optimize first for personal authority, while allowing a
    future organization only through explicit later re-architecture.

### D. Domain and longevity

11. **Which domain posture is acceptable?** Rank `.com`, `.id`, `.dev`, `.io`,
    `.me`, `.research`, or another TLD; state any maximum annual renewal budget.
    **Recommendation:** prioritize a clean `.com` when credible, then a concise
    `.id` or `.dev`; avoid a poor or misleading name merely to obtain `.com`.

12. **Do you already own domains or public handles that must be preserved or
    redirected?** List them, but do not provide passwords or private tokens.

13. **How long should this name plausibly remain valid?**  
    **Recommendation:** require a ten-year test: credible if AI terminology
    changes, if Engineering becomes more prominent, and if the OS metaphor
    becomes less fashionable.

14. **Name three brands, authors, fictional system names, callsigns, or words
    whose naming quality you admire—and three you dislike.** The goal is to
    understand sound and structure, not to imitate them.

## 5. Controlled naming workflow after answers

1. Convert responses and Phase 2 evidence into a naming brief and rejection
   lexicon.
2. Generate a broad candidate universe across eponymous, derived-nickname,
   conceptual, technical-root, and invented-word territories.
3. Apply automatic rejection before subjective scoring.
4. Score survivors using Document 01's weighted model and a ten-year stress
   test.
5. Present a diverse longlist, then a maximum eight-name shortlist with honest
   strengths, weaknesses, pronunciation, and brand architecture.
6. For finalists only, research current domain availability, renewal cost,
   search collisions, organizations/products, handles, and preliminary
   trademark/confusing-similarity risk using authoritative live sources.
7. Select the masterbrand and domain direction through Product Owner approval;
   no purchase occurs automatically.
8. Integrate the choice into the wordmark contract, metadata, attribution,
   environment labels, voice, domain redirects, and ADR.
9. Cross-check every Phase 2 deliverable and lock Phase 2.

## 6. Candidate rejection rules

A candidate is removed before scoring if it:

- falsely implies a laboratory, company, institution, or team;
- hides Raihan's authorship or makes professional attribution awkward;
- depends on `AI`, `cyber`, `quantum`, `tech`, `labs`, `OS`, or hacker tropes to
  sound distinctive;
- is primarily an imitation or close allusion to a fictional property;
- is hard to hear, spell, type, pronounce, or use in an email address;
- traps the identity inside only AI, mining, engineering, or today's aesthetic;
- has an obvious harmful/comic meaning in a relevant language;
- is confusingly close to an established product, person, organization, or
  mark; or
- can work only with an unavailable, excessively expensive, or misleading
  domain.

- the Phase 2 requirement/decision/open-question cross-check is complete; and
- Raihan explicitly locks Phase 2 with any bounded deferrals recorded.
