# 18 — Publishing, Rights, and Version Governance

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Gate:** 2.4 — Publishing and Flagship Design

## 1. Controlled lifecycle

1. Private idea
2. Private work in progress
3. Content-type and schema validation
4. Research or engineering evidence review
5. Confidentiality, artifact rights, and license review
6. Staging preview in full OS, direct-link, reading, and mobile structures
7. Link, accessibility, interaction, and fallback checks
8. Explicit Raihan approval
9. Publish; update search, sitemap, feeds, and declared relationships
10. Revise, supersede, archive, or withdraw with recorded reason

No private-state name is automatically exposed as a public status.

## 2. Validation layers

- schema and required-field validation;
- canonical path/slug uniqueness;
- internal relationship integrity;
- external and artifact link checks;
- type-specific evidence gate;
- confidentiality/sanitization gate;
- rights/license compatibility;
- accessibility and static fallback;
- search/metadata/structured-data preview; and
- publication inventory and environment placement.

Errors block. Warnings require explicit acceptance in the publication record.

## 3. Rights matrix

Rights are declared separately for:

- prose;
- source code;
- data;
- models/weights;
- reports and spreadsheets;
- figures/diagrams;
- photographs/media; and
- third-party quotations or embedded material.

A page-level open-source statement does not automatically license every
artifact. Closed-source artifacts remain explicitly closed.

## 4. Version policy

- `1.0` marks first public version;
- major version changes alter material claim, method, scope, or conclusion;
- minor version changes improve explanation, add evidence, correct bounded
  errors, or update artifacts without replacing the central claim;
- typo/style corrections may update modified date without public version bump
  when they cannot affect meaning;
- every material version records date, change, reason, and evidence impact.

Version semantics may later be encoded differently by the selected authoring
stack, but public behavior must remain equivalent.

## 5. Corrections and supersession

### Correction

Keep the URL, update version, and disclose the material correction.

### Supersession

Keep the old page accessible, label it Superseded, explain why, and link to the
successor. The successor links back when lineage matters.

### Withdrawal

When legal, confidentiality, integrity, or safety reasons require removal,
retain a minimal tombstone at the canonical URL when safe. State the category
of reason without exposing protected information.

## 6. Staging approval record

The approval record contains:

- content ID and proposed version;
- validation result and accepted warnings;
- research/engineering gate outcome;
- confidentiality and rights decisions;
- preview surfaces reviewed;
- reviewer/approver and timestamp;
- artifact versions/checksums when useful; and
- publish, block, reclassify, or defer decision.

## 7. Single-author operability

- validation messages identify field and remediation;
- templates supply structure, not filler claims;
- local and staging preview use the same canonical content source;
- no database editing is required;
- routine publishing updates derived indexes automatically;
- rollback restores a known published version; and
- monthly maintenance can focus on content rather than manual index repair.

## 8. Collaboration routing

- Research: research collaboration, review, artifact, or repository discussion;
- Engineering Case Study: project brief or professional contact;
- Project: project/open-source route according to rights;
- Coming Soon: prefilled `Discuss this topic`;
- Essay/Note: low-emphasis general route; and
- persistent Collaboration Center: always available.

Form contents are private operational data and never become content metadata or
analytics dimensions.
