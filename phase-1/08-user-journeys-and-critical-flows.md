# 08 — User Journeys and Critical Flows

**Document status:** Draft v0.1

## Journey 1 — AI professional evaluates research

**Entry:** Search result, shared research URL, or Central OS.

1. Visitor sees the research title, significance, status, and accessible summary.
2. Visitor identifies the question, contribution, and evidence type.
3. Visitor enters technical depth.
4. Visitor inspects methodology, assumptions, evaluation, uncertainty, and
   limitations.
5. Visitor manipulates a guided interactive element when useful.
6. Visitor opens report, repository, data, or reproduction material.
7. Visitor explores related research or opens Collaboration Center.

**Success:** The visitor can explain what was claimed, why it may be credible,
and where to verify it.

**Recovery:** If interaction fails or is unsupported, static content still
communicates the core result and evidence links remain available.

## Journey 2 — Cost engineer evaluates a case study

1. Visitor enters Engineering environment.
2. Visitor selects a case by problem, method, or domain.
3. Visitor reads sanitized context and scope.
4. Visitor inspects basis, assumptions, sources, methodology, and validation.
5. Visitor sees explicit confidentiality and data-transformation notes.
6. Visitor reads lessons and transferable method.
7. Visitor opens Collaboration Center.

**Success:** Professional credibility increases without exposing client or
proprietary database information.

## Journey 3 — General reader develops understanding

1. Visitor enters through Central OS or a shared article.
2. Visitor reads the plain-language summary.
3. Terms, equations, or concepts are introduced progressively.
4. A guided interaction makes an abstract relationship concrete.
5. Visitor optionally continues into technical detail.
6. Visitor follows related essays or research.

**Success:** The reader gains a correct high-level understanding without being
forced through advanced detail.

## Journey 4 — Open-source contributor inspects an artifact

1. Visitor finds an open research/project page.
2. Open/closed status and license are immediately visible.
3. Visitor opens the correct versioned repository.
4. Visitor reads reproduction and contribution information.
5. Visitor follows repository issues/discussions rather than an on-site
   comment system.

**Success:** Contribution does not depend on guessing the repository, license,
version, or communication channel.

## Journey 5 — Prospective client proposes a project

1. Visitor gains trust through research or case-study evidence.
2. Visitor selects Collaborate from the persistent shell or page CTA.
3. Visitor chooses structured form, email, professional profile, GitHub, or
   another enabled channel.
4. Structured form requests only information needed for an initial assessment.
5. Visitor reviews privacy/handling notice and submits.
6. Visitor receives accessible success confirmation or actionable error.

**Success:** Raihan receives enough non-sensitive context to assess fit.

## Journey 6 — Power user navigates by terminal

1. Visitor opens terminal.
2. Help shows available commands and examples.
3. Visitor searches, lists, opens, switches environment, or enters reading mode.
4. Result opens the same canonical destination used by visual navigation.
5. Command errors suggest valid alternatives without losing state.

**Success:** Terminal is faster and meaningful, not a scripted decoration.

## Journey 7 — Raihan publishes content

1. Raihan selects the correct content template.
2. Raihan writes English canonical content and structured metadata.
3. Content validation checks required fields, links, dates, status, and license.
4. Research or case-study gates run as applicable.
5. Raihan previews the item in staging, reading mode, mobile, and full OS.
6. QC issues are corrected.
7. Raihan approves publication.
8. Sitemap, feed, related content, and search index update.

**Success:** Publication is repeatable by one author without direct database
editing.

## Journey 8 — Visitor arrives on mobile

1. Visitor reaches a direct content URL or mobile Central OS.
2. The simplified shell communicates identity and domain.
3. Content loads without heavyweight desktop window behavior.
4. Visitor reads, searches, opens evidence, or collaborates.

**Success:** Mobile is complete for discovery, reading, evidence, and contact
even though the desktop experience remains richer.

## Critical usability observations for internal validation

Raihan should attempt each journey without implementation guidance and record:

- first expected action;
- hesitation or confusion;
- mistaken clicks/commands;
- whether the current environment is understood;
- whether evidence can be found;
- whether a direct URL feels complete;
- whether collaboration is discoverable;
- whether visual spectacle delays the task;
- whether reading mode restores sustained focus.
